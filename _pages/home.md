### Welcome!

Control of Cyber-Physical Systems

Our research focuses on developing advanced control strategies for complex cyber-physical systems operating in uncertain and dynamic environments. By combining theoretical control design, simulation-based experimentation, and machine-learning techniques, we create controllers that adapt and learn in real time. Current efforts include the development of learning-based autopilots for aircraft and multicopters that can expand operational envelopes and maintain stability during sudden environmental or dynamic changes. These methods have broad applications in autonomous vehicles, industrial robotics, and flight control systems. Complementing this work, we also design computationally efficient, data-driven algorithms for state and parameter estimation in complex dynamic systems such as atmospheric and flow models, enabling real-time prediction and control with improved accuracy and performance.

### About me

I am an Assistant Professor of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC), where I direct the Estimation, Control, and Learning Laboratory (ECLL).
My research focuses on developing data-driven and learning-based control and estimation techniques for complex mechanical and aerospace systems.
At ECLL, we integrate tools from linear and nonlinear system theory, optimization, and machine learning to create adaptive algorithms that enable robust, real-time decision-making in uncertain and dynamic environments. Our work spans a range of applications, including robotics, autonomous systems, and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs).
